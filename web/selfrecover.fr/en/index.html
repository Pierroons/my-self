SelfRecver

Recover your account. No email, no SMS.

An open-source account recovery protocol. You keep a single word in your head: it never leaves your browser, only a site-bound fingerprint reaches the server. No email provider and no phone carrier in the loop.

The problem

"Forgot your password?" → we email you a link.

Except email is a third party. If your inbox goes down, gets hacked, or the provider shuts down or bans you, you lose access to every account that depends on it. SMS has the same flaw, with SIM swapping on top. SelfRecover takes email and phone out of recovery.

How it works

What you know becomes your key.

Three ideas, all readable in the code.

◐

Split knowledge

The word alone proves nothing. Neither does the algorithm. Brought together in your browser, they yield an HMAC-SHA256 fingerprint the server can check: one of the two factors of level 2.

⑃

Site-bound

In hostname mode, the fingerprint depends on the address your browser reads: a copy of the page served elsewhere derives a fingerprint that is worthless here. The price: changing the address costs existing accounts their level 2. A service can pick a fixed label instead, which survives an address change but no longer protects against phishing.

∅

What the server sees

Your word, never: it only receives its fingerprint. The passphrase and the password do pass through it when they are used. In its database it keeps only Argon2id hashes.

Three recovery levels

Three distinct ways to prove it's you.

Moving to the next level is your choice, never an automatic escalation: each level asks for something else, and only you know what you have left.

Level 1

Passphrase

Your username and a six-word passphrase, drawn by the server from the EFF diceware list (≈ 77.5 bits). It is kept on paper and works only once: each use gives you a new one.

L1
Level 2

Word + code

Your memorized word and one of your ten single-use paper codes. No username to type: the code finds the account. Optionally, the device you enrolled replaces the code, never the word.

L2
Level 3

The human

Lost everything? You present a bundle of facts, and a human administrator decides. Never a score or an automatic reset, and a refusal never touches the account. If approved, you set your secrets again yourself.

L3

Limits

What SelfRecover does not cover.

A security protocol is also judged by what it declares out of its reach.

  • Root access to the server bypasses the whole protocol.
  • A rigged page that captures your word as you type it stays out of reach, as for any protocol that lives in the browser.
  • Reusing your word on a malicious site hands it over: per-site derivation prevents cross-matching databases, not that leak. A word unique to each service remains safer.
  • A weak word remains attackable: rate limits and the human level 3 slow it down, the derivation does not protect it.
  • No external security audit to date. An internal adversarial audit was carried out; red team feedback is welcome.

Open down to the code

Nothing to trust. Everything to verify.

SelfRecover is free software: self-host it, audit it, integrate it into your service. A PHP library that needs only standard extensions, the browser deriver shipped with it, and a ready-to-use database schema.

It installs with Composer from the repository; it is not yet published on Packagist or npm. The README describes the protocol, its threat model and how to integrate it.

What it changes for you

  • No more reset link sitting in an inbox.
  • No more phone number handed to the service.
  • In hostname mode, a copy of the site gets only a fingerprint that is useless on the real one.
  • If everything is lost, a human decides, not an algorithm.

The MySelf ecosystem

Your key opens the door. SelfDataGuard guards what's behind it.

SelfRecover gives you back access. SelfDataGuard encrypts at rest the data the service hands to it. The two libraries do not call each other: the service passes the same secrets to both.

▨

Encrypted at rest

Fields handed to SelfDataGuard leave a dump encrypted: one of your secrets is needed to reopen them. During an open session, the server holds the key in memory.

⧉

Several locks

Your data key is sealed under each of your secrets: password, memorized word, passphrase. Each one opens it alone: when the service seals them all, forgetting one does not cost you your data.

⚷

The same idea for your disk

SelfRecover-LUKS unlocks a LUKS2 encrypted volume with a recovery passphrase unique to each machine, separate from your word: neither one opens the other.
